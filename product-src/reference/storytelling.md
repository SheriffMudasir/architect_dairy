# Storytelling for 60–120 s explainers

## Shape that holds attention
1. **Hook with the twist in the first sentence** — the surprising end state, stated plainly.
   "In 1882, Barcelona started building a church. 144 years later, it still isn't finished."
2. **The person and the promise** — who, where, what was meant to happen (name card).
3. **The vision** — the design appears (model rises / ghost plan draws on).
4. **Friction** — critics, site, politics (quote card, photo of the place).
5. **The hard number** — one shocking figure (big counter).
6. **The break** — cancelled, scrapped, burned, demolished (stamp + camera shake).
7. **Aftermath / replacement** — what happened next (time-lapse, second model).
8. **An emotional beat** — a death, a tribute (music dips, mono photo).
9. **Ironic coda** — the twist after the twist.
10. **Outro line that mirrors the hook.**

## Rules
- 12–16 beats, 190–270 words. One or two short sentences per beat. One idea per beat.
- Spoken verbatim: no brackets, no directions. Numbers written for reading (`1926`, `forty-seven`).
- Headlines: 2–5 acts; each a `lead` + coloured `accent` phrase that sums up the act.
- `em`: 2–4 caption highlights per beat — names, numbers, the twist word.
- Prefer concrete nouns and dates over adjectives. Cut every sentence the visuals already say.
- Quotes only when sourced; otherwise "is said to have…" + label "Attributed to…" — and flag it.
- End on an image the viewer remembers (the ghost above the real building, the empty seats).

## Topic picking
Great topics have: a twist, a building you can model in low-poly, real photos under CC/free
licences, and verifiable numbers. Unbuilt designs, construction sagas, demolitions, rivalries,
"the architect never saw it finished", record-breakers with a catch, and timely anniversaries.
